# Demo voice-over script

About 2:45 of picture, read at a calm ~150 words per minute. Each block matches one scene; the seconds are the
scene's length. Rule: a scene lasts at least words ÷ 2.5 + 0.5 s. Captions on screen stay short; the voice carries
the explanation. Every claim is backed in the product or by a cited source.

| # | Scene (seconds) | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook (4) | One phone. One serial number. Two stores. |
| 2 | Problem (7) | Counterfeits are a four-hundred-and-sixty-seven-billion-dollar trade. In smartphones, one sale in eight is lost to fakes. |
| 3 | Gap (6) | Verification exists, but it's optional and separate from paying. So nobody uses it. |
| 4 | Idea (6) | TrustLedger makes authenticity a condition of payment. |
| 5 | How it works (10) | The brand's data stays with the brand. PayPal's stays with PayPal. Questions travel to the data, and only private aggregates come back. |
| 6 | Scenario 1 (18) | Store A scans serial X123. The brand confirms it, and only then does PayPal create the order, with the verification ID inside. The buyer approves, and the receipt is brand verified. |
| 7 | Scenario 2 (14) | Store B scans the same serial. It was never shipped there. The checkout stops before any money moves: no PayPal order exists. |
| 8 | Scenario 3 (20) | The brand gets a case. An AI agent investigates on its own, asking each node questions. Never a single record. Its findings name where the evidence diverges. |
| 9 | Business case (11) | And here's how it pays for itself. Brands spend over a hundred and fifty billion dollars a year on market insights, mostly slow, self-reported surveys. |
| 10 | Scenario 4 (22) | With TrustLedger, a brand asks who buys its phones and gets an answer from verified purchases in moments. Privacy-protected, with confidence intervals, and a budget that every question spends. |
| 11 | Guarantee (8) | Every buyer is mathematically protected across all of a brand's questions. No one is identifiable. |
| 12 | Onboarding (15) | Brands run one verification API. PayPal runs the checkout gate and the private node. A store adds one SDK call, generated by APIMatic. AI shopping agents can call the same check. |
| 13 | Close (7) | Trust at checkout for buyers. Intelligence for brands. A stronger PayPal network. |

## Accuracy notes (must stay true to the product)

- "one sale in eight" = 12.9% (EUIPO/ITU, 2015 data); "four hundred and sixty-seven billion" = OECD/EUIPO 2025,
  2021 data. Sources and years are on the cards.
- "over a hundred and fifty billion dollars a year on market insights" = ESOMAR, 2024 data; it is the insights
  industry, not surveys alone, so "mostly slow, self-reported surveys" is a characterisation, not a number.
- Scenario 3 narration must not name a store or distributor: the live agent's finding varies by run.
- "mathematically protected" = zCDP ρ 0.25 ≈ ε 4.2 (δ 1e-8) per buyer, lifetime budget per brand; the card shows it.
  "No one is identifiable" is the design goal the guarantee bounds; the card's footnote gives the red-team evidence.
- "generated by APIMatic": only once the APIMatic SDK exists; otherwise "a store adds one API call".
- "AI shopping agents can call the same check": the MCP server in `mcp/` (openapi-mcp-generator).
